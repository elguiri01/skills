# Grow Search Visibility

Build search visibility by finding unmet user questions and publishing the strongest verifiable answer, then making it technically discoverable and independently corroborated. Treat rankings, AI citations, referral traffic, and conversions as related but distinct outcomes.

## Operating rules

- Browse for current, site-specific evidence. Prefer first-party platform guidance for technical claims.
- Ask only for inputs that materially change the work. A URL is enough to begin a public audit. Ask about market, audience, conversions, CMS, analytics access, and constraints when needed.
- Distinguish observed facts, user-provided facts, inferences, and recommendations.
- Never guarantee ranking, indexing, inclusion, citation, traffic, or revenue.
- Never invent authors, credentials, tests, reviews, customers, statistics, affiliations, or editorial independence.
- Reject doorway swarms, near-duplicate query permutations, cloaking, hidden redirects, expired-domain or site-reputation hijacking, paid links presented as editorial, fake UGC, review/schema cosplay, and scaled low-value publishing.
- Do not confuse correlation in an answer-engine snapshot with a causal ranking factor.
- Do not treat domain-authority scores from third-party tools as search-engine metrics.

## Select the task depth

Choose the smallest useful mode:

1. **Rapid audit:** public evidence, top issues, and 30-day priorities.
2. **Full strategy:** technical, content, entity, corroboration, distribution, and measurement plan.
3. **Execution:** implement approved changes in the user's site or repository, then validate.
4. **Experiment:** construct a fixed query battery, record baselines, change one meaningful variable, and compare results over time.

Do not mutate a live site, publish, contact people, or create accounts unless the user explicitly authorizes execution.

## Workflow

### 1. Define success and scope

Record the website, business model, audience, country/language, conversion event, primary entities, competitors, current data access, constraints, measurement window, and target outcome.

If the user provides no private data, continue with a public audit and label data gaps.

### 2. Establish a baseline

Inspect representative page types, not just the homepage. Check:

- robots directives, crawler access, canonicals, status codes, XML sitemaps, rendering, internal links, duplicate or orphan URLs, indexability, pagination, international targeting, and obvious performance issues;
- page purpose, search intent, factual accuracy, freshness, authorship, editorial ownership, disclosure, original evidence, citations, and conversion path;
- titles, headings, answer clarity, tables/lists where useful, descriptive links, images/video, accessible markup, and structured data that matches visible content;
- brand/entity consistency across the site and legitimate external profiles;
- current ranking URLs, cited sources, SERP composition, and answer-engine sources for a sample query set.

Never infer indexation solely from a `site:` query. Prefer Search Console or webmaster data when available.

### 3. Build a neutral query universe

Generate real user questions from products, entities, tasks, objections, constraints, and decisions. Cover category discovery; how-to and mechanics; cost, eligibility, timing, risk, and requirements; comparisons and alternatives; brand checks; specific use cases, segments, locations, and low-volume edge cases; and follow-up questions an answer engine may fan out into.

Group queries by intent and underlying information need. Consolidate phrasings that one strong page can satisfy. Do not recommend one thin page per keyword variation.

### 4. Find citation vacuums

For each cluster, inspect conventional results and, where tools permit, multiple answer engines. Record who ranks or is cited, source type, recency, first-party evidence, weaknesses, and whether the result actually resolves the task.

Prioritize clusters where user and commercial value are meaningful, existing answers are weak, the site has a real right to win, one canonical resource can resolve the need, and legitimate corroboration is possible.

Use the model and templates in [references/opportunity-and-measurement.md](references/opportunity-and-measurement.md).

### 5. Design the source worth citing

Choose the correct page type: original study, comparison, calculator/tool, dataset, guide, FAQ section, product/service page, case study, glossary, location page, or updateable reference.

Require a content specification with:

- exact audience and decision;
- direct answer or conclusion near the top;
- unique contribution such as first-party data, a reproducible method, expert analysis, firsthand testing, worked examples, or a maintained dataset;
- clear definitions, assumptions, limitations, dates, and update owner;
- verifiable author/reviewer identity and relevant experience when applicable;
- primary-source support for factual claims and visible commercial disclosures;
- scannable headings, concise answer blocks, useful tables/lists, and descriptive anchors;
- internal links from relevant hubs and to supporting evidence;
- structured data only when supported by visible content and platform rules;
- a conversion path that does not obscure the answer.

For YMYL topics, raise the evidence and review threshold. Do not let conversion copy outrun the evidence.

### 6. Build legitimate corroboration

Treat third-party presence as evidence distribution, not borrowed trust. Select surfaces where the audience genuinely participates: reputable trade or local publications, professional associations, original-research pitches, expert interviews, podcasts, webinars, authentic review platforms, relevant communities, useful video, and real partner or institution pages.

Match every contribution to a genuine editorial or community need. Disclose sponsorships and affiliate relationships. Never mass-produce platform-hosted doorway pages or mirror near-duplicate articles across hosts.

### 7. Secure discovery and accessibility

Confirm that important pages are crawlable, indexable, canonical, internally linked, usable, and rendered with meaningful HTML. Maintain current XML sitemaps. Use supported submission mechanisms such as Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, or IndexNow when appropriate.

Check relevant crawler controls separately. For ChatGPT search eligibility, confirm that `OAI-SearchBot` is not unintentionally blocked. Keep search crawling and model-training controls conceptually separate.

### 8. Prioritize the roadmap

Score actions by expected impact, confidence, effort, time to signal, dependency, and risk. Put blockers before content expansion. Present top findings with evidence, immediate fixes, 30/60/90-day actions, named page targets, owner assumptions, and stop/continue criteria.

Avoid false precision. Use ranges or High/Medium/Low when inputs are weak.

### 9. Measure and iterate

Track crawl/index coverage, impressions, rankings, clicks, qualified sessions, conversions, brand mentions, referring-domain quality, answer-engine citation rate, cited URL, visible citation order, answer accuracy, referral traffic, and content freshness.

Use a fixed, versioned prompt battery. Preserve engine, date, locale, login state, model/mode, and capture method. Repeat samples because outputs vary. Compare query-level results, not only blended averages.

Attribute cautiously. Prefer staged rollouts, matched page groups, or before/after tests with clear confounders.

## Deliverable standard

Lead with the decision and highest-impact opportunities. Include enough evidence for the user to verify every important recommendation. For a full strategy, provide:

1. executive diagnosis;
2. baseline and evidence gaps;
3. query/citation-vacuum map;
4. technical fixes;
5. page-level content plan;
6. corroboration and distribution plan;
7. prioritized 90-day roadmap;
8. measurement scorecard;
9. risks, assumptions, and items requiring access.
