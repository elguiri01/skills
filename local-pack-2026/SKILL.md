# Local pack, 2026

Skill 21 runs the checks. This says what the checks are for. Official APIs
only, as Skill 21 already requires; the reasons got stronger this month.

## The levers, in order

Joy Hawkins's answer to "rebuild local SEO from zero": **reviews, on-site SEO,
keyword in the business name.** Reviews are first.

The Whitespark 2026 survey order (expert opinion, not a measurement): primary
category; proximity; keywords in the business name; a physical address in the
city searched; open at the time of search; high rating; address shown rather
than hidden; additional categories; number of native reviews; pin placement.

### Reviews

Three things, benchmarked against the top three for each target term:

- **Velocity**: reviews per month.
- **Volume**: total.
- **Rating**: one client moving from 4.5 to 4.6 saw a lift (direction only, no
  base). Above 4.7 is the working target.

Singh, from client grids: the business with more reviews in the last 90 days
ranked better in 7 of 9 HVAC matchups, and one competitor going from 8 to 42
reviews as its only change gained 7.9 average grid positions. Small samples,
consistent direction.

The decision rule Sterling Sky uses in client reports: "competitors get five a
month, you get one". If the client cannot match the velocity, **retarget a
smaller keyword set they can win** rather than promising the big one.

Ask every customer. Ask them to add a photo. Asking them to mention the
service and the town is fine; scripting the wording, rewarding the review, or
sending only the happy ones to Google is not.

### The page the profile links to

The cheapest win in Singh's data. Optimising only the URL linked from the
profile moved average grid rank from 15.5 to 9.53 on one listing and 12.9 to
9.76 on another. Three listings, no period given.

On that page: title, H1 and H2s that name the service and place; the business
name and phone in the footer; licence number, years in business and
certifications as plain text; the equipment brands actually installed; embedded
reviews; keyword links from the homepage to service pages. Consolidate thin
second and third-tier service pages that get no traffic.

### Granular detail

For Gemini in Maps ("Ask Maps", "Know before you go"), publish very specific
long-tail detail even where keyword tools show no volume. Hawkins's example: an
appliance repair shop that published error codes saw rankings lift across all
its services. One case. It is also information gain by another name: nobody
else in the town has written it down.

### Name and address

- A keyword in the business name works. The only clean route is the legal
  name or a registered trading name. Stuffing the profile name is a suspension
  risk and is not ours to do for a client.
- Service descriptors in the name beat location descriptors (one agency's
  observation).
- **A visible address ranks better than a hidden one.** Sterling Sky's study:
  rankings fell when the address was hidden and recovered when shown.
- Service-area businesses rank worse. Their biggest home-services strategy is
  a **real, staffed second office** with branded signage where the demand is.
  One electrician put the gain at $500k in a year (his own estimate). Real and
  staffed is the whole condition.
- Check the client actually sits inside the city boundary they want to rank
  in.

### What does not earn its time

- **Google Posts.** No measurable return; Sterling Sky stopped. Skill 21 lists
  "recent posts" in its health check. Keep it as an observation, drop it as a
  task. If a client insists, tag every post link and let the click count
  decide.
- **Links, for the map.** Many built, almost no local pack movement. They move
  organic.
- **Citation consistency.** Reported to matter much less than it did.
- **Scaled location pages.** Singh: penalised hard in local, AI-written or
  not. The same warning as `information-gain`.
- **EXIF data on photos.** Asserted to help. Google is widely reported to
  strip it.

## Five formats, not one

Google now runs five local formats that compete for the same space: the
classic local pack, the AI local pack, three-pack ads, Local Services Ads and
organic. Report presence in each.

The AI local pack shows fewer businesses in 88% of the markets Sterling Sky
looked at: three listings become about one and a half, the second half hidden.
No base stated. Their Yext study (over 8 million screenshots, not read) puts
organic results 48% more volatile since the May core update.

## Trackers no longer see what customers see

This corrects Skill 21, which treats the geogrid as the reading.

- In the week before 14 November a US local agency saw logged-in mobile users
  getting ads plus an AI local block and **no local pack**, while incognito
  showed the old layout. DataForSEO's screenshot for the same query returned
  the traditional three-pack with no ads.
- Many trackers' screenshots leave out ads, especially logged out.
- Places Scout grid reports were running up to about 20 hours late, so the
  time of day is wrong and closed businesses distort the pack. That makes
  Skill 21's business-hours rule necessary and not sufficient: check when the
  run actually executed.
- Single rank positions are no longer reportable. If tracking, sample each
  keyword dozens of times across the grid so the order shows through the
  noise. It is expensive and working less well.

So: spot-check on a phone, logged in, from the client's market (ask the
client), and treat grids as a trend line. Geogrids cost real money per run;
weekly at most, as Skill 21 says.

## Report leads

Rankings and attribution are both breaking, so the report rests on leads over
time.

- **Call tracking is the backbone.**
- **Ask every inbound lead** where they heard of the business and what they
  searched. Train whoever answers the phone.
- Tag every profile link, and expect AI local pack clicks to land on either
  the profile or the site and sometimes to show as organic.
- **Ring the client's number yourself out of hours.** The HighLevel talk opens
  with a client who blamed call tracking when nobody was answering.

One anecdote worth telling a nervous client: a business that lost about 90% of
its organic traffic to an old link penalty had its best year ever on its
Business Profile alone.

`client-value-reporting` has the report structure.

## Third-party lists

Hawkins calls established local "best of" lists the biggest current win, and
says for AI it is mentions that matter, not links. Kiekbusch: a small local
business can dominate local AI answers with one list or award. Get the client
onto established lists that exist independently. Paid inclusion is fine where
the list labels it.

This is also the commercial case for our own directory: a list people and
engines already consult, with a labelled premium tier. See
`conference-intel-2026-11/plays.md`.

One hard line from the lunch conversation: a lawyer publishing "we are the
best" lists about themselves has FTC and state bar exposure. Regulated
professions get the conservative version of everything here.

## Local Services Ads

For every eligible client. Every field complete; calls answered within five
seconds, around the clock, which in practice means an answering service; at
least ten new photos a week; fast review replies. Mark the profile open 24
hours only if calls really are covered. Hawkins: add an ads person rather than
skip ads; ad conversion is the best it has been.

## Diagnosing a drop

Singh's routine, which fits Skill 21's data sources:

1. For each lost grid point, who holds it now.
2. What changed for them: a new listing, a category change, a moved pin, a new
   or changed website, a jump in reviews.
3. What changed for the client: category, hours, address visibility, the
   linked page.

Find who took the spot before theorising about an algorithm.

## Lead-gen listings and rank-and-rent

Hawkins has not seen an exact-match lead-gen listing rank in an AI Overview.
She calls such listings extremely volatile (60% of sites or profiles can be
removed overnight) and her clients report them. Bulk-verified listings were
purged. Treat any rank-and-rent revenue as revocable income and never as an
asset to borrow against. A directory of real businesses, which is what the
`local-directory-strategy` skill builds, is a different thing.

## Refused

Every one of these was recommended on stage, and two speakers reported what
happened next.

- **Bought reviews.** A law firm rose on them and fell when Google deleted
  them. Leo: bought reviews killed some of his own listings. Hawkins relays a
  per-business trust score that, once broken, stops real reviews sticking.
- **Review gating.**
- **Click manipulation**, by phone farms, paid clicks to the profile URL or
  vendors. Holds only while it continues.
- **Aged or rented profiles, verification bypasses, separate profiles per
  service line.**
- **Seasonal category switching** to chase demand: a re-verification or
  suspension risk on a client's only profile.
- **Automating the profile interface.** Official APIs only.

## What this skill will not claim

That map rank drives AI visibility. Singh asserts it and nobody tested it.
Hawkins says the opposite about the signals: that what moves AI answers and
what moves the map have diverged.
